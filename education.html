Education · UPC + UPF

Engineering & Economics

I study Industrial Technologies and Economic Analysis, a four-year joint bachelor’s degree taught in English at Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya and Universitat Pompeu Fabra.

Two perspectives, one degree

Engineering helps me understand how systems work. Economics helps me think about incentives, trade-offs, organizations and the decisions that shape them.

Studying both together has made me interested in problems that don't fit neatly into a single discipline — especially when technical possibilities and business decisions intersect.

What the programme brings together

Engineering & quantitative sciences

Mathematics, mechanics, physics, industrial technologies, programming, modelling and optimization.

Economics & decision-making

Micro- and macroeconomics, econometrics, statistics, game theory, incentives and business analysis.

These are areas covered across the programme, not a claim that every subject has already been completed.

How it shapes my thinking

I've become more comfortable working with quantitative information, defining problems clearly and combining different viewpoints. Those habits matter both in consulting work and when making practical decisions for Guspira.

In particular, I enjoy connecting what is technically possible with what is economically sensible and operationally realistic.

Before university

At INS Moisès Broggi in Barcelona (2021–2023), I completed the bilingual International Baccalaureate alongside the Spanish Scientific-Technological Baccalaureate, and received a distinction in the university entrance exams (PAU).

Tools I've worked with

Data & analysis: Python, Pandas, R, Excel, Power BI, Minitab.

Engineering & modelling: MATLAB, SolidWorks and ANSYS.
